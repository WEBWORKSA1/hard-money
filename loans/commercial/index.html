Commercial hard money fills the gap when a property or sponsor does not fit bank or agency boxes: value-add multifamily with low occupancy, a retail center with lease-up risk, an office conversion, a hotel repositioning, or land with entitlements in progress. Leverage is lower than residential and the diligence is heavier, but a decision in days rather than months is often what wins the deal.

Typical uses

  • Acquisition of under-performing or vacant commercial assets.
  • Cash-out to recapitalize a partnership or fund tenant improvements.
  • Discounted payoff of a maturing CMBS or bank loan.
  • Land acquisition with entitlement risk, or horizontal development.
  • Special situations: partner buyouts, tax liens, bankruptcy exits.

How commercial lenders underwrite

Expect a focus on as-is value, stabilized value and the business plan that connects them. Sponsors need relevant experience, a credible budget and timeline, and liquidity for reserves. Third-party reports (appraisal, environmental Phase I, property condition) add two to three weeks and $5,000–$15,000 in cost. Larger loans are often structured with an interest reserve, a completion guarantee and springing recourse.

Presenting a deal

A one- to two-page executive summary wins faster responses: property and location, purchase price or payoff, as-is and stabilized values with support, sources and uses, business plan with milestones, exit, sponsor bio and liquidity. Submit it through the funding request and attach rent rolls and T-12 operating statements where available.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum commercial hard money loan?
Most commercial-focused private lenders start at $500,000 to $1 million; residential-focused lenders will do 5–10 unit multifamily from about $250,000.
Is commercial hard money non-recourse?
Sometimes at lower leverage (55–60% LTV) with strong collateral; most loans carry a personal guarantee or at least bad-boy carve-outs.
