A bridge loan is short-term debt that “bridges” the gap between buying a property and either selling it or refinancing into long-term financing. In private lending the term covers everything from a 70% LTV loan on a stabilized rental that needs a fast close, to a multifamily acquisition that cannot qualify for agency debt until occupancy improves.

When a bridge loan makes sense

  • Speed: the seller needs to close in 10 days and a bank cannot get there.
  • Condition or occupancy: the property is vacant, partially leased or needs light work, so permanent lenders decline it until it is stabilized.
  • Borrower profile: your tax returns are complex, you are a foreign national, or you own more financed properties than agency guidelines allow.
  • Cash-out: you want to pull equity from a free-and-clear property to fund the next acquisition.
  • Auction or REO purchases where the contract allows no financing contingency.

Cost and structure

Expect 8% to 11% interest-only, 1 to 2 origination points, and a 6- to 24-month term, often with a built-in extension option. Larger commercial bridge loans ($5M+) price off SOFR plus a spread and may require an interest reserve escrowed at closing. Because bridge loans are sized on current value, the appraisal matters more than on a flip: a conservative as-is value directly cuts your proceeds.

Exit planning

Lenders approve bridge loans on the credibility of the exit. For a rental, line up a DSCR takeout before you close the bridge and confirm its seasoning requirement (many DSCR lenders now refinance on the new appraised value with no seasoning if the rehab is documented). For a sale, support the pricing with recent comps. For commercial, know the occupancy or debt-yield threshold the permanent lender needs and the time it will take to reach it.

Frequently asked questions

Is a bridge loan the same as a hard money loan?
Bridge loan describes the purpose (short-term, transitional); hard money describes the source and underwriting style (private capital, asset-based). Most private bridge loans are hard money loans, but banks also make bridge loans with full income underwriting.
Can I get a bridge loan on a primary residence?
Business-purpose private lenders generally cannot lend on an owner-occupied home because of consumer-lending regulations. Owner-occupied bridge loans exist but come from licensed mortgage lenders under different rules.
How much can I cash out with a bridge loan?
Typically up to 65–70% of appraised value on a free-and-clear investment property, less any payoff of existing liens.
