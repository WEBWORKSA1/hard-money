Fix and Flip Loans

Short-term, interest-only financing that covers the purchase and 100% of the renovation budget for 1–4 unit properties you plan to resell.

A fix and flip loan is the workhorse of hard money lending. It is a short-term, asset-based loan — usually 6 to 18 months, interest-only — that finances the acquisition of a distressed 1–4 unit property and the renovation budget, with the lender underwriting primarily on the deal (purchase price, rehab scope and after-repair value) rather than on your tax returns.

How a fix and flip loan is structured

Lenders size the loan with two caps and you get the lower of the two:

  • Loan-to-cost (LTC): a percentage of purchase price plus rehab budget — commonly 85% to 92.5% for experienced flippers, 80% for first-timers, and up to 100% of the rehab portion.
  • Loan-to-ARV: a ceiling of 70% to 75% of the after-repair value as supported by an appraisal or broker price opinion.

The purchase portion funds at closing. The rehab portion sits in a holdback and is released in draws as work is completed and inspected — typically 24 to 72 hours after an inspection. Most lenders charge interest only on funds actually drawn.

Worked example. Purchase $220,000, rehab $60,000, ARV $380,000. Total cost $280,000. At 90% LTC the loan is $252,000; at 75% ARV the cap is $285,000. The loan is $252,000, so you bring roughly $28,000 plus points and closing costs. At 10% interest-only, the carry is about $2,100 per month while the project runs.

What it costs in 2026

Pricing is tiered by experience and credit. New investors with a 660 FICO can expect roughly 10.5% to 11.5% and 2 points; investors with 10+ completed flips and 720+ credit see 8.25% to 9.5% and 1 to 1.5 points. Expect a document or processing fee of $1,000 to $2,000, an appraisal or valuation fee, and draw-inspection fees of $150 to $300 each. Most fix and flip loans carry no prepayment penalty, which matters because the average flip is sold in 5 to 7 months.

Requirements

  • Non-owner-occupied property (business-purpose loan). Most lenders require you to borrow through an LLC or corporation.
  • Credit score floor of 600 to 660 depending on the lender; a soft pull is common at pre-qualification.
  • Liquidity: proof of funds for the down payment, closing costs and 3 to 6 months of interest reserves.
  • A credible scope of work with a line-item rehab budget and contractor bids.
  • Exit strategy: resale comps, or a pre-qualification for a DSCR refinance if you plan to hold.
  • Experience is not required, but it moves every dial: leverage, rate, points and speed.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Close in days, not 45+ days — you can compete with cash offers.
  • Rehab budget financed; distressed properties qualify.
  • No tax returns or DTI calculation; underwriting is deal-based.
  • Interest-only payments keep carry low during construction.
  • No prepayment penalty at most lenders.

Cons

  • Rates are 2–4 points above conventional and you pay origination points.
  • Short term: a stalled project can force an expensive extension.
  • Draw process adds friction and inspection fees.
  • Personal guarantee is standard even when borrowing through an LLC.
  • Leverage caps mean you still need real cash in the deal.

Frequently asked questions

How fast can a fix and flip loan close?
Direct lenders routinely close in 5 to 10 business days once title and valuation are back; some advertise 24- to 72-hour closings for repeat borrowers with a clean file. The realistic pacing item is the appraisal or BPO.
Do I need experience to get a fix and flip loan?
No. First-time investors are financed by most national lenders, but at lower leverage (75–80% LTC), higher rates and more points. Completing even one or two documented flips materially improves pricing.
Can I get 100% financing on a flip?
Some lenders offer 100% of purchase for very experienced borrowers, and 100% of rehab is common, but total leverage is still capped by the 70–75% ARV rule. “No money down” marketing usually means cross-collateralizing another property or a profit-share structure.
What happens if I cannot sell before the loan matures?
Most lenders offer one or two extensions of 3 to 6 months for a fee of 0.5 to 1 point. Beyond that, refinancing into a 30-year DSCR rental loan is the standard exit.
