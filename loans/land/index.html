Land is the hardest asset class to finance because it produces no income and its value depends on what can be built. Banks rarely lend on it; private lenders will, at conservative leverage, when the path to an exit — permits, construction financing or a resale to a builder — is clear.

Raw vs. entitled land

Raw acreage with no utilities or zoning approvals typically caps at 35–50% LTV. Entitled, shovel-ready infill lots in strong metros can reach 60–65% and are often rolled directly into a construction loan. Lenders will want a survey, zoning letter, utility availability and, ideally, approved plans.

What to prepare

  • Purchase contract and title commitment.
  • Appraisal or broker opinion supported by lot sales, not just acreage comps.
  • Entitlement status with timeline to permits.
  • Development budget and a construction lender's term sheet or pre-approval.
  • Sponsor liquidity; land loans frequently require interest to be prepaid.

Frequently asked questions

Can I finance land and construction with one loan?
Yes — many construction lenders fund the lot acquisition or payoff as the first draw, which is usually cheaper than two separate loans.
