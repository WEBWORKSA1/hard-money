FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Short, direct answers. Each links to a deeper guide where one exists.

What is a hard money loan?
A hard money loan is a short-term real-estate loan from a private lender or fund that is secured by the property and underwritten mainly on the asset and the deal — purchase price, renovation budget and after-repair value — rather than on the borrower’s income. Terms typically run 6 to 24 months, interest-only, at rates of roughly 8% to 13% plus 1 to 3 origination points.
How fast can I get funded?
Direct lenders commonly issue a term sheet within 24 hours and close in 5 to 10 business days. Repeat borrowers with a clean file and no appraisal requirement can close in 2 to 3 days. The usual pacing items are title work and the valuation.
What credit score do I need?
Most national hard money lenders require 600 to 660. Credit affects your pricing tier and maximum leverage more than approval itself. Regional private lenders may have no minimum if you bring 30%+ equity.
How much down payment is required?
Typically 10% to 20% of total project cost for experienced investors and 20% to 25% for first-timers, plus closing costs and points. Total leverage is also capped at 65–75% of after-repair value.
Can I use a hard money loan for my primary residence?
Generally no. Hard money lenders make business-purpose loans on non-owner-occupied property. Owner-occupied loans fall under consumer-mortgage regulations that most private lenders do not operate under.
Do I need an LLC?
Most lenders require or strongly prefer that you borrow through an LLC or corporation, both for liability reasons and because entity loans are exempt from certain consumer-lending and usury rules. You will still sign a personal guarantee.
What are points?
Origination points are an upfront fee equal to a percentage of the loan amount — one point is 1%. Hard money loans usually carry 1 to 3 points, paid at closing or occasionally rolled into the loan.
Is interest charged on the whole loan or only what I draw?
It depends on the lender. Many fix-and-flip lenders now charge interest only on funds actually disbursed (the purchase portion plus released draws). Others charge on the full committed amount from day one — ask before you sign.
What is the difference between hard money and private money?
Hard money usually refers to institutional or fund-backed private lenders with published programs. Private money refers to individuals lending their own capital with negotiated terms. Both are asset-based; private money is often cheaper and more flexible but harder to source.
What happens if my loan matures before I sell or refinance?
Most lenders grant one or two extensions of 3 to 6 months for a fee of 0.5 to 1 point. If you cannot extend, the standard exit is a 30-year DSCR refinance. Plan the exit before you borrow.
Are hard money loans regulated?
Business-purpose loans are exempt from most federal consumer-mortgage rules (TILA, RESPA, ATR), but lenders are still subject to state licensing and usury laws, fair-lending laws and fraud statutes. Several states — notably California, Arizona, Nevada, Oregon and Utah — require licenses for lenders or brokers making these loans.
