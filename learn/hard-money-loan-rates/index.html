Hard Money Loan Rates and Points Explained (2026 Update)

HMBy the Hard.Money editorial team·Published 2026-10-30·8 min readFact-checked

Advertiser disclosure: we may be compensated by lenders featured on this site; this does not influence our editorial content. Learn more.

Key takeaways

  • Fix and flip rates cluster between 8.25% and 11.5% in 2026; DSCR rental loans 6%–8%; construction and commercial 9%–13%.
  • Points (1–3%) and fees often matter more than rate on a 6-month loan.
  • Pricing is driven by experience, credit, leverage, property type, state and the lender’s capital source.
  • Always compare the all-in cost over your realistic hold period, not the headline rate.

What drives your rate

  1. Experience tier. The biggest single factor. RCN Capital's published matrix, for instance, moves roughly 150 basis points between a first-time flipper and a 10+ flip veteran.
  2. Credit score. Bands at 660, 680, 700, 720 and 740 each shift pricing 12–50 bps at most lenders.
  3. Leverage. Every 5% of LTC or LTARV you give back is worth roughly 25 bps.
  4. Property type and condition. Heavy rehab, 2–4 units, mixed-use and rural property price higher than a cosmetic single-family flip.
  5. State. Judicial-foreclosure states (NY, NJ, FL, IL, OH) and strict-usury states (TX) carry a premium.
  6. Loan size. Loans under $150,000 often carry rate or point minimums; loans over $1 million get negotiated.
  7. Capital source. Lenders funded by warehouse lines and securitizations price tighter than those funded by high-yield investor notes, but are less flexible.
  8. Market rates. Hard money moves with the 2-year Treasury and the cost of warehouse credit, lagging by a quarter or so.

Why points matter more than rate on short loans

On a 6-month hold, 2 points cost the same as 4% of annual interest. Compare two offers on a $300,000 loan held 6 months: Offer A at 9.5% and 2.5 points costs $14,250 interest + $7,500 points = $21,750. Offer B at 10.75% and 1 point costs $16,125 + $3,000 = $19,125. The “higher rate” lender is cheaper by $2,625. The crossover point here is about 9 months; past that, Offer A wins. Run your hold-period scenario in the calculator.

Other fees to compare

  • Document/underwriting fee: $995–$2,000
  • Appraisal or BPO: $300–$900 (sometimes credited back)
  • Draw inspection: $150–$300 per draw
  • Extension: 0.5–1 point per period
  • Interest on undrawn funds: some lenders charge it, some do not — this alone can be worth 1%+ of total cost on a heavy rehab
  • Payoff/reconveyance fees: $100–$500
  • Minimum interest (e.g., 3 months guaranteed) on some programs

How to negotiate

Get three term sheets on the same deal and share them. Ask for a point reduction in exchange for a slightly higher rate if you expect a quick exit, or the reverse for a long hold. Offer a larger down payment to drop a tier. Bundle: lenders will sharpen pricing for borrowers who commit to several deals a year or who bring the DSCR takeout to the same shop. And ask about interest on undrawn rehab funds — it is often negotiable even when the rate is not.

Frequently asked questions

Are hard money rates negotiable?
Within a lender’s matrix, yes — particularly points, fees and the undrawn-interest policy. Competing term sheets are your leverage.
Why are hard money rates so high?
The capital behind them costs 7%–10% itself, the loans are short and labor-intensive to service, and the collateral is often distressed. The spread pays for speed and risk.
Do rates differ by state?
Yes. Judicial-foreclosure and strict-usury states carry a premium of 50–150 bps for comparable borrowers.
