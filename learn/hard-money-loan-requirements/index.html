Hard Money Loan Requirements: What Lenders Actually Check in 2026

The five pillars of a hard money approval

Unlike a bank, a hard money lender does not run you through a rigid matrix. Underwriters weigh five things together, and a strong deal can carry a weak borrower — to a point.

1. The property and the deal

Non-owner-occupied residential (1–4 units), small multifamily, mixed-use or commercial in a market the lender serves. The underwriter wants to see a purchase price below market, a realistic rehab budget with contractor bids, an ARV supported by three to six comparable sales within a mile and six months, and an all-in cost that leaves a 20%+ margin to ARV. Rural property, manufactured homes, condos in litigation and properties with environmental issues are common exclusions.

2. Equity (skin in the game)

Expect to contribute 10–15% of total cost as an experienced investor and 20–25% as a first-timer, plus points and closing costs. Lenders also cap total exposure at 65–75% of ARV. Gift funds are rarely accepted; partner capital is fine if documented. Cross-collateralizing another property can substitute for cash.

3. Liquidity and reserves

You must show the down payment, closing costs and typically 3–6 months of interest payments in verifiable accounts — bank, brokerage, sometimes retirement accounts at a discount. Lenders look for sourced and seasoned funds (30–60 days of statements) and will ask about large recent deposits.

4. Credit

Minimums run 600–660 at national lenders; a few regional lenders have none. Credit sets your pricing tier: each 20–40 point band moves rate and leverage. Recent mortgage lates, open bankruptcies, active foreclosures and unpaid judgments are the items that actually cause declines. Medical collections and old charge-offs are usually ignored.

5. Experience

Most lenders use tiers such as 0, 1–2, 3–5 and 6+ completed projects in the last 36 months. Proof is a settlement statement from purchase and sale, or a deed plus refinance statement for holds. Each tier adds roughly 2.5–5% of leverage and shaves 25–75 basis points. If you have none, a licensed contractor or an experienced partner on the guarantee can bump you a tier.

Entity and guarantor requirements

Nearly every institutional lender requires the borrower to be an LLC, LP or corporation, with all members owning 20%+ signing a personal guarantee. You will need articles of organization, an operating agreement, an EIN letter and a certificate of good standing. Single-member LLCs formed days before closing are fine. Foreign nationals can qualify at many lenders with a U.S. entity, a U.S. bank account and larger reserves.

Document checklist

  • Executed purchase contract (or payoff statement for a refinance)
  • Scope of work and rehab budget, with contractor bids and license/insurance
  • Two months of bank statements for all accounts used to close
  • Government ID and a short investor bio or track-record sheet
  • Entity documents and EIN
  • Credit authorization (soft pull at term sheet, hard pull before closing)
  • Insurance binder: builder's risk or vacant-dwelling policy naming the lender
  • Exit documentation: resale comps or a DSCR pre-qualification letter

Red flags that get deals declined

  • Owner-occupied or intended owner-occupancy, including a family member living there
  • Loan to an individual rather than an entity in states where that triggers consumer rules
  • Rehab budget below what the scope obviously requires (“lipstick budget”)
  • ARV supported only by active listings or comps from a better neighborhood
  • Unsourced cash, or funds appearing the week of closing
  • Contract flips or wholesale assignments without lender disclosure
  • Title issues: unreleased liens, probate, HOA super-liens

How to strengthen a marginal file

  1. Bring more equity — the single fastest fix.
  2. Add an experienced co-guarantor.
  3. Reduce the ask: a smaller loan relative to ARV changes the lender's risk math.
  4. Pre-order a BPO so the lender's valuation holds no surprises.
  5. Present a one-page deal summary; organized borrowers get better terms.

Frequently asked questions

Do hard money lenders verify income?
Rarely. They verify liquidity (bank statements) and sometimes ask for a personal financial statement, but there is no debt-to-income calculation.
Is an appraisal required?
Most lenders require an appraisal or broker price opinion; a few waive it for low-leverage loans or repeat borrowers.
Can I use retirement funds for reserves?
Many lenders count IRA/401(k) balances at 50–70% of value toward reserves, but not toward the down payment unless withdrawn.
