Hard Money vs. Conventional Loan: Which Fits Your Deal?

HMBy the Hard.Money editorial team·Published 2026-10-30·7 min readFact-checked

Advertiser disclosure: we may be compensated by lenders featured on this site; this does not influence our editorial content. Learn more.

Key takeaways

  • Conventional wins on rate; hard money wins on speed, property condition and leverage against cost.
  • The right test is whether the deal’s margin comfortably absorbs the extra financing cost.
  • Many investors use both: hard money to acquire and renovate, conventional or DSCR to hold.

When hard money is the right tool

  • The property will not pass a conventional appraisal (no kitchen, roof damage, code violations).
  • The seller requires a 14-day close or you are buying at auction.
  • You need the rehab budget financed.
  • You own more than 10 financed properties, or your tax returns do not support the payment.
  • You are buying through an LLC and want the loan in the entity's name.

When conventional is the right tool

  • The property is move-in ready and you intend to hold it for years.
  • You have 30+ days and W-2 or stable self-employment income.
  • You are under the financed-property limit and have 20–25% down.
  • The deal's return depends on the lowest possible payment, not on speed.

Worked example: a $300,000 flip

Purchase $300,000, rehab $60,000, ARV $480,000, 6-month project. Hard money at 10% with 2 points on a $324,000 loan (90% LTC): $16,200 interest + $6,480 points + $2,000 fees = $24,680 financing cost, and you fund $36,000 of cost plus the points. Conventional is unavailable because the property is distressed and the rehab is not financed — you would need $120,000 cash for 20% down plus the full $60,000 rehab. The hard money deal nets roughly $480,000 − $360,000 cost − $24,680 financing − $38,000 selling costs ≈ $57,000 on about $45,000 of cash deployed. The financing cost is real, but the deal does not exist without it.

Using both: the two-loan structure

Most rental investors eventually run a hybrid: hard money to acquire and renovate, then a refinance into a 30-year DSCR loan or, for borrowers with strong income and few properties, a conventional investor loan. The hard money loan's cost is then just the “admission price” to a property that could not be bought any other way. Read our BRRRR financing guide for the sequencing.

Frequently asked questions

Can I refinance a hard money loan into a conventional loan?
Yes, if you qualify on income and the property is finished. Expect a 6-month seasoning requirement for cash-out on the new value with agency loans; DSCR lenders are often more flexible.
Is hard money ever cheaper than conventional?
Almost never on rate. It can be cheaper in total when it lets you buy at a deeper discount (cash-like offer) or avoid a lost deal.
