Hard Money Loans With Bad Credit: What Is Actually Realistic

HMBy the Hard.Money editorial team·Published 2026-10-30·7 min readFact-checked

Advertiser disclosure: we may be compensated by lenders featured on this site; this does not influence our editorial content. Learn more.

Key takeaways

  • Institutional lenders floor at 600–660; regional private lenders and individuals will go lower with 30%+ equity.
  • Expect 10.5–14% and 2–4 points; the collateral and the deal carry the file.
  • Recent mortgage lates, open bankruptcy and active foreclosure are the true deal-breakers, not the score itself.
  • A co-guarantor, cross-collateral or a smaller first deal are the fastest fixes.

What “asset-based” really means for credit

Hard money lenders advertise that they lend on the property, and they do — but nearly all of them pull credit and use it to set pricing tiers and leverage. The score is a proxy for how you handle obligations when a project goes sideways. The practical landscape in 2026: national lenders (Kiavi, RCN, Lima One, LendingOne and peers) generally require 620–660; several (Easy Street, Groundfloor) publish a 600 floor; regional fund lenders and individual private lenders may have no stated minimum and lend on equity alone. Our bad-credit loan page summarizes typical terms.

What lenders actually read in a low-score report

  • Deal-breakers: mortgage payments 30+ days late in the last 12 months, an open bankruptcy, an active foreclosure or deed-in-lieu within 24–36 months, unpaid judgments or tax liens that would attach to the property.
  • Manageable: medical collections, old charge-offs, high utilization, a thin file, a discharged Chapter 7 older than 12–24 months, a short sale older than 2 years.

How to compensate

  1. Equity. Move from 10–15% down to 25–35%. At 60–65% of ARV, many lenders stop caring about score.
  2. Co-guarantor. An experienced partner with 700+ credit on the guarantee changes the tier for the whole file.
  3. Cross-collateral. Equity in another property can replace cash and reassure the lender.
  4. Smaller, simpler project. A cosmetic flip in a liquid market is easier to approve than a gut rehab.
  5. Reserves. Show 6–12 months of interest in the bank.
  6. A clean story. A one-paragraph letter of explanation with documentation (medical event, divorce, business closure) is routinely accepted.

What it costs

A 580–620 borrower on a $200,000 loan should budget 11–13% and 3 points: roughly $6,000 in points and $1,850–$2,150 per month. Over a 6-month flip that is about $18,000 in financing, versus $12,000 for a 720+ borrower at 9% and 1.5 points. The deal must have at least that extra $6,000 of margin — many do.

A 60-day plan to move up a tier

  1. Pull all three reports; dispute errors (takes 30 days).
  2. Pay revolving balances below 30% of limits — the fastest score lift, posted within one cycle.
  3. Settle small collections under $500 with pay-for-delete where possible.
  4. Do not open new accounts; do not close old ones.
  5. Ask to be added as an authorized user on a seasoned, low-utilization card.

Gains of 30–60 points in 60 days are common for files dragged down by utilization and small collections — enough to cross a 620 or 660 threshold.

Avoid these “bad credit” traps

  • Upfront “commitment fees” from lenders you have not vetted — legitimate lenders charge appraisal and processing at most before closing.
  • Advance-fee brokers who guarantee approval.
  • Loans structured to individuals on owner-occupied homes marketed as “business purpose” — this is where predatory lending hides.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a no-credit-check hard money loan?
Some individual private lenders do not pull credit, but virtually every institutional lender does. Treat “no credit check” advertising with suspicion.
Does a hard money loan help rebuild credit?
Usually not — most are made to LLCs and are not reported to personal credit bureaus.
