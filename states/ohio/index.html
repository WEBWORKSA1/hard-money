The Ohio market

Ohio offers some of the best cash-flow math in the country for BRRRR: sub-$200,000 acquisitions, rents that clear the 1% rule, and strong DSCR exit demand. Columbus is the growth story; Cleveland and Cincinnati are yield plays.

Licensing

The Ohio Residential Mortgage Lending Act applies to loans primarily for personal, family or household purposes; business-purpose loans to entities are generally outside its scope.

Usury and interest-rate rules

Ohio’s 8% statutory rate yields to written agreements on business loans and loans above $100,000, so business-purpose hard money is effectively uncapped when documented correctly.

Foreclosure and lender risk

Ohio uses judicial foreclosure as its primary method, with a typical timeline of judicial — commonly 6 to 12 months. Judicial process means a lender must sue to recover collateral, which lengthens recovery and raises cost — lenders compensate with lower leverage (often 5 points lower) and higher rates than in non-judicial states.

What is different here

Judicial foreclosure and lower price points mean lenders require more equity and often impose $75,000–$100,000 loan minimums that exclude some Cleveland and Dayton deals. Point-of-sale inspection ordinances in several Cleveland suburbs affect flip timelines.

FAQ

Is hard money legal in Ohio?
Yes. Business-purpose private loans secured by real estate are legal in Ohio, subject to the state's licensing and usury rules summarized above. Lenders typically require an entity borrower and non-owner-occupied property.
How fast can a hard money loan close in Ohio?
Most residential deals close in 5–10 business days once title and valuation are back. Because Ohio uses judicial foreclosure, lenders may require a few extra days of title and insurance diligence.
What do hard money loans cost in Ohio?
Indicatively 10% – 13% with 2 – 3 points for fix-and-flip loans in 2026, depending on experience, credit and leverage.
